# Rift Ascendant canon locks

This document governs Rift Ascendant rules across campaigns. A campaign can add a specific exception, but cannot silently redefine these system rules. When sources disagree, use this order:

1. Current explicit canon locks in this document and later recorded decisions.
2. Current canonical compendium data and runtime implementation.
3. Current in-app sourcebook text.
4. Legacy migrations, archived drafts, and historical comments.

Update the compendium, persistence, runtime, UI, tests, and in-app sourcebook together when a lock changes. Historical migrations remain historical unless a new migration must repair data they still seed. The retired PDF book pipeline and any PDFs it produced are archival, not authorities.

## Regents and Sovereigns

- **RA-1 — Regent Resonance.** One character-wide pool is shared by all unlocked Regents. Its maximum at levels 1–20 is `1,1,2,2,2,3,3,3,4,4,4,5,5,5,6,6,6,7,7,8`. Regent-acquired tier 5 Powers and Techniques cost 1; tiers 6–7 cost 2; tiers 8–9 cost 3. A Long Rest restores the pool; a Short Rest does not. The pool replaces a Regent acquisition's native per-rest charges rather than stacking with them. A first unlock starts it full; a second unlock does not refill it. On a level increase, add the increase in maximum to current points, preserving points already spent.
- **RA-2 — Acquisition provenance.** Each character Power and Technique grant records its acquisition kind (`job`, `path`, `regent`, `rune`, `feature`, `manual`, or `other`) and canonical source. Regent grants also record their canonical Regent and unlock IDs. The same canonical ability may have distinct acquisition grants and use economies. Display `source` text is never the runtime authority. Backfill old Regent grants only from unambiguous unlock and approved-pick records; unresolved rows keep their existing use rules pending review.
- **RA-3 — Regent HP.** At every character level, add one maximum Regent Hit Die to Job HP. A d10 Regent therefore adds +10 at each level: +30 total by level 3, including retroactive catch-up on unlock. A d12 Regent adds +120 total by level 10. Level-up adds one new die contribution, not the cumulative total again. Job HP already accounts for VIT; never apply VIT a second time. A higher maximum HP does not itself heal current HP.
- **RA-4 — Full gestalt.** The progression is Job + Path + Regent. The Job is the primary class, the Path is its specialization, and an earned Regent is a second full class overlay. Regent level equals character level once unlocked, and all progression through that level applies immediately.
- **RA-5 — Unlock.** A Warden or co-Warden presents exactly three distinct canonical Regents and the player selects one. There is no random, stat-selected, level-gated, partial, percentage-based, or player-created fourth choice. The unlock is binary. A character can have at most two Regents.
- **RA-6 — Known abilities.** Martial and half-caster Regents use the known-count progression `2,2,3,3,3,4,4,4,5,5,5,6,6,6,7,7,7,8,8,8` for each authored Power and Technique progression. Full-caster Regents keep their spell/cantrip progression. An above-level-1 unlock immediately receives owed selections through Warden-curated catch-up. Later Regent Power/Technique choices are selected by the player from canonical tiers 5–9. High-tier effects are not weakened by low character level; Resonance is their use limit.
- **RA-7 — Feature independence.** Regent class features keep their authored action and recovery rules. Resonance applies only to Regent-acquired Powers and Techniques, never to class features, Job/Path features, Job resources, or spell slots.
- **RA-17 — Sovereign vocabulary.** Regent names an individual class overlay. Gemini Protocol is the fusion of two Regents. Sovereign names the resulting fused state and package; it is never a synonym for Regent, and no other game object uses the word. The Holy Knight mount formerly called Sovereign Steed is the Pantheon Steed.
- **RA-18 — AI isolation.** AI may run only during initial Sovereign synthesis from Job, Path, Regent A, and Regent B, through the dedicated Sovereign endpoint. Every other generator and AI feature is removed: the Rift, NPC, Random Event, Treasure, Directive, and art generators; AI appearance and backstory drafting; Homebrew AI Draft; Guild and Marketplace description generation; initiative narration; the Rollable Table and Relic enhance buttons; and the offline asset-generation launchers. This applies to every executable project path, including local authoring tools. Loading, leveling, combat, resource use, rests, viewing, export, and import use saved deterministic data and make no AI calls.
- **RA-19 — Complete creation.** Save the complete Sovereign definition at creation: identity, theme, manifestation, traits, features, resources, mechanics, ancestry and contributions from all four sources, and eight milestone abilities at levels 1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 14, 17, and 20. Later levels never ask AI to invent missing abilities. Legacy records may be read for compatibility without silently manufacturing content.

## Rift life and companions

- **RA-8 — Biological Anomalies.** Anomalies are native biological organisms in Rift ecologies. The Bureau/Ascendant label describes their difference from Earth fauna, not automatic hostility. Species, anatomy, and ecology are distinct from an encounter's disposition, behavior toward a party, and current hostility. An Anomaly can be predator, prey, scavenger, social, symbiotic, neutral, defensive, or hostile. Nonbiological Rift hazards and native peoples are classified on their own terms.
- **RA-9 — Character-owned companions.** A wild Anomaly uses its canonical species stat block. A tamed creature, a bonded creature, and every mount belong to the character that holds them, as companions do in other tabletop RPGs. The Warden does not approve, hold, or lock a character's companion, and the owner edits its sheet. The Add Companion catalog is the list of creatures a character can take as a companion. The canonical Anomaly ID identifies a species; a companion instance ID identifies one living creature, so two creatures of one species stay distinct. There are no per-creature companion profiles and no per-turn command tracking. A player tells the Warden when a character mounts or dismounts; the app does not track riders.
  - **Retired roster.** The campaign tamed roster and the per-character tamed-anomaly table are retired. Each creature moved to its primary handler's sheet (else its current controller's, else its tamer's) with its identity intact; the old rows are read-only history. Character files that still list `tamed_anomalies` import those creatures as companions.
- **RA-10 — One scaled version.** Each species has one version, scaled to the owning character's level (L, 1–20). PB = 2 + floor((L − 1) / 4). Scaling applies to Anomaly companions, to mounts linked to an Anomaly stat block, and to the catalog mounts flagged combat capable: Mana-Touched Wolf, Bureau Warhorse, Bureau K9 (Mastiff-class), Mountain Patrol Bear, and Pantheon Steed. Utility mounts, guild allies, and custom companions keep their saved stats.
  - **Hit points.** The creature has L Hit Dice, each at its maximum value. The die is the stat block's Hit Die (`12 (1d10 + 6)` → d10), or by size when none is authored: Tiny d4, Small d6, Medium d8, Large d10, Huge d12, Gargantuan d20. No VIT is added. A level-up raises maximum HP without healing current HP.
  - **Damage.** Every damage roll keeps its die size: the stat block's die, or the size die for an authored natural attack. The dice count is 1 at levels 1–4, 2 at 5–10, 3 at 11–16, and 4 at 17–20. Attack damage adds PB and drops the stat block's flat bonus. Save-based effects roll dice only. Dice that are not damage, such as durations and recharge ranges, are unchanged.
  - **Defenses.** AC = 10 + rank tier + floor((L − 1) / 4). Attack bonus = 2 + rank tier + PB. Save DC = 8 + rank tier + PB. Rank tiers are E 0, D 1, C 2, B 3, A 4, and S 5; a missing rank counts as D. A linked mount uses its own catalog rank and its Anomaly's Hit Die.
  - **Actions.** A scaled creature uses its stat block's traits, actions, bonus actions, reactions, and legendary actions; lair actions stay with a wild creature's lair. A linked mount adds its own abilities. A combat-capable mount without a stat block uses its natural attacks: Mana-Touched Wolf Bite (piercing, d8), Bureau Warhorse and Pantheon Steed Hooves (bludgeoning, d10), Mountain Patrol Bear Bite (piercing, d10) and Claws (slashing, d10), and the Bureau K9's authored d6 Bite.
  - **Rests.** A companion rests with its character, by the character's rules. On a Short Rest its owner may spend its Hit Dice; each die heals one roll of its Hit Die, with no VIT added. On a Long Rest it regains all HP and half its Hit Dice (minimum 1), and its conditions end as the character's do. A companion that keeps its saved stats has no Hit Dice to spend; a Long Rest still restores all its HP.
  - **Examples.** A d10 Anomaly has 50 HP at level 5. A Mana-Touched Wolf has 40 HP and a 2d8 + 3 bite at level 5. A 1d6 claw deals 1d6 + 2 at level 1, 2d6 + 3 at level 5, 3d6 + 4 at level 11, and 4d6 + 6 at level 17.

## Materials, crafting, and research

- **RA-11 — Bureau regulation.** The Bureau can regulate certification, legality, sale, registration, transport, contracts, reporting, controlled materials, and market access. Bureau affiliation or certified equipment is not a universal prerequisite for physical harvesting, crafting, field repair, research, experimentation, Inscription, or material use. A specific regulated procedure may state a narrower requirement.
- **RA-12 — Material taxonomy.** Material families are Anomaly Biological, Essence, Rift Botanical, Rift Mineral, Relic Material, and Technical. Source ranks E/D/C/B/A/S normally map to material grades Basic/Quality/Rare/Exotic/Legendary/Regent, but rank and grade remain separate stored facts. Formula inputs have Consumed, Incorporated, or Catalyst roles; successful crafting does not automatically destroy every input.
- **RA-13 — Disciplines.** Ordinary disciplines are Blacksmithing, Alchemy, Enchanting, and Field Engineering. Inscription is a separate specialized procedure using the same inventory. Other procedures, including Field Survival, are not additional ordinary disciplines. Research and biological adaptation extend the common project/inventory engine.
- **RA-14 — Research.** Retain Field Standard, Known, Experimental, and Proven states. An Experimental procedure has increased difficulty until proven, learns iteratively from attempts, becomes Proven through a successful prototype, and gains mastery through repeated successful production. A biological procedure's own formula defines mutation, rejection, and other risks; there is no universal biological failure rule.

## Combat and parity

- **RA-15 — Critical damage.** A critical hit deals maximum normal damage dice + rolled critical dice + flat modifiers once. For `1d8 + 4`, the result is `8 + 1d8 + 4`. Additional authored critical dice are rolled. One shared damage resolver applies the rule.
- **RA-16 — Vulnerability.** Typed vulnerability multiplies damage by two in the shared damage application pipeline. It is a mechanical modifier, not just descriptive Anomaly text.
- **RA-20 — Terminology.** Active code, compendium entries, UI, documents, and the in-app sourcebook use Job, Path, Regent, Gemini Protocol, Sovereign, and biological Anomaly consistently. Historical text may remain visibly archival; it must not be consumed as current rules. Retire active Monarch, Regent-as-subclass, highest-stat Regent, and monster-as-universal-Anomaly usage.
- **RA-21 — Parity.** A system correction includes authoritative data, runtime enforcement, persistence, UI, rest behavior, export/import, tests, the in-app sourcebook text, and an active-wording search. Do not declare a rules change complete merely because one Markdown document was edited.

## Jobs, Paths, and features

- **RA-22 — Feature save DC.** When a Job or Path feature calls for a saving throw without stating its DC, the DC is the Job save DC: 8 + proficiency bonus + the Job's key ability modifier. Key abilities are STR for Destroyer and Berserker; AGI for Assassin and Striker; INT for Mage, Revenant, and Technomancer; SENSE for Herald, Summoner, and Stalker; and PRE for Esper, Contractor, Holy Knight, and Idol. Powers use the same DC. A Regent feature uses the Regent save DC: 8 + proficiency bonus + the modifier of the Regent's first primary ability. Spells use the spell save DC. A feature that states its own DC keeps it.
- **RA-23 — Feature options.** A feature that offers options names every option. The player chooses when the character gains the feature, and the sheet records the choice as its own feature entry. A later feature that grants more options adds to the earlier picks. Unless a feature says otherwise, whenever the character gains a level in that Job, the player may replace one option from that feature with another.
- **RA-24 — Created and commanded creatures.** A creature that a feature creates, summons, or commands uses the stat block that feature authors. It shares its creator's proficiency bonus and acts on the creator's initiative, immediately after the creator. It takes the Dodge action unless the creator uses a bonus action to command it or the feature lets it act freely. It lasts for the feature's duration, until it drops to 0 hit points, until the creator dismisses it (no action), or until the creator uses the feature again. Its sheet is a custom companion on the creator's character that keeps its saved stats: it has no Hit Dice, does not rest, and regains hit points only as its feature allows.
- **RA-25 — Path attributes.** A Path lists a primary and a secondary attribute as build guidance. A Path grants no ability score increases.
- **RA-26 — Ability Score Improvements.** A character gains each Job ASI when it levels up into that ASI level. A character created or imported above 1st level keeps its entered scores: the app adds no retroactive ASI and never infers past choices from current scores. The exact legacy Mage marker (INT +2, PRE +1) converts to INT +2, SENSE +1; any other marker is left unchanged for its owner to adjust.
- **RA-27 — Retired names.** A retired Job, Path, or Regent name resolves only through the aliases its canonical entry declares, and imports store the canonical id and name. The retired Regent names Shadow, Flame, Titan, Dragon, Architect, and Transfiguration Regent, and Frost Sovereign, are not aliases and resolve to nothing; active text uses Umbral, Radiant, Steel, Destruction, Spatial, Mimic, and Frost Regent.
- **RA-28 — Canon sources.** Rift Ascendant canon is authored in this repository, in the order of authority at the top of this document. Provenance cites repository files and fields; there is no separate printed edition and no page citation.
